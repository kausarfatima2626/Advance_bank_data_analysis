01 — Exploratory Analysis

What separates a "yes" from a "no"

Four cross-sections of the campaign data: how contact channel, prior outcomes, age, and occupation shift the odds of a subscription.

Subscription Rate by Contact Method

Cellular outreach converts at a markedly higher rate than landline or unknown-channel contact — a strong candidate for prioritizing call lists.

Prior Campaign Outcome

Customers who subscribed in a previous campaign are far more likely to say yes again — history is one of the strongest signals available.

Subscriptions by Age Band

Conversion isn't linear with age — younger (under-30) and retirement-age (60+) segments both outperform the middle-career band.

Top Occupations by Conversion

Students and retirees lead on conversion rate, even though blue-collar and management roles dominate on raw call volume.

03 — Takeaways

Reading the ledger

The findings worth carrying into the next campaign design.

  • 01

    Call duration is the strongest single predictor — and the trap

    Longer calls correlate strongly with subscriptions, but duration is only known after the call ends, so it can't be used to decide who to call next. It's kept for analysis, dropped for live prediction.

  • 02

    Past success predicts future success

    A customer who subscribed in a prior campaign converts at several times the base rate. Segmenting past responders into a priority tier is a low-effort, high-yield change.

  • 03

    Class imbalance shapes every metric that matters

    With roughly 1 in 9 customers subscribing, raw accuracy is misleading — precision, recall, and ROC-AUC on the minority class are what actually judge the model.

  • 04

    Channel and season both move the needle

    Cellular contact and specific months (notably outside the busy spring calling season) show higher conversion — timing the campaign matters as much as targeting it.
